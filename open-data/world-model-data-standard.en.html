Foreword

The value of embodied intelligence data depends on whether it faithfully, continuously, and traceably describes interactions between an agent and the physical environment and provides reliable learning signals. Training data for a World Action Model (WAM) must support both future world-state prediction and action generation consistent with those predictions. Data development must therefore center on the observation–action–outcome relationship, ensuring trustworthy individual interaction records, dataset coverage of relevant scenarios, and an effective scale usable for training.

This specification establishes coordinated requirements for data quality, data distribution, and data scale. Quality ensures reliable learning signals; distribution supports generalization across tasks, environments, and embodiments; scale supplies sufficient effective interaction experience. These dimensions must be developed together, using independent evaluation to establish evidence linking data improvements to model performance.

Prepared by the Motubrain Team at Shengshu Technology from its embodied data governance practice, this draft corporate technical specification is open for industry consultation. It gives collectors, processors, annotators, providers, and model users a shared quality vocabulary and acceptance basis. The main text defines data development and evaluation requirements. Appendices A and B list basic checks and execution rules, and specialized checks, respectively. Appendix C provides a dataset card, acceptance report template, status review reference, and minimal applicability configuration example; Appendices D and E provide an acceptance checklist and delivery requirements by data type. All appendices are informative. Conformity assessment depends on requirements and evidence for the declared use, rather than counts of checks or tools.

1 Scope

This specification defines embodied data requirements for training and evaluating world models, particularly WAMs. It covers collection design, raw recording, processing and conversion, annotation, dataset organization, quality inspection, delivery, and performance validation.

It primarily applies to ego, physical-robot, and UMI-style data for object manipulation and related mobile manipulation tasks. Pure navigation, legged locomotion, and similar tasks may follow the general principles but require additional state, contact, and task evaluation requirements. All three data types may contribute to training, but their observation capabilities, action sources, supervision targets, and transfer limitations must be declared separately.

Data typeMain learning valueUsage limitations
egoHuman manipulation processes, object interactions, task semantics, and environmental changesHuman hand trajectories are not equivalent to robot control commands; video-only data supports only the observation or auxiliary supervision targets it can substantiate
Physical robotRobot states, control actions, execution feedback, and physical interaction outcomesDistinguish control commands, target states, and measured states; declare embodiment, control mode, and latency
UMI-styleManipulation trajectories, gripper behavior, and diverse environmental interactions recorded by handheld interfacesDeclare pose estimation, gripper measurement, action derivation, and robot mapping methods; recorded trajectories do not inherently satisfy the target robot's execution constraints

UMI originates from research on the Universal Manipulation Interface. Here, “UMI-style” means handheld manipulation interface data with an explicit motion and gripper recording contract. Research source

Simulation, synthetic, and model-generated data may supplement other sources. Their origin, generation method, and validation results must be identified separately. They must not be presented as real-world recordings or counted toward real-world collection scale. Internet video alone does not automatically satisfy the embodied interaction data requirements in this specification.

2.2 Requirement language and document authority

“Shall/shall not” (and equivalent mandatory forms “must/must not”) denote a requirement; “should/should not” denote a recommendation; “may” denotes an allowed option. A pass means only that requirements within the declared scope are satisfied; it does not establish suitability for every task or guarantee model performance.

The main text sets the criteria for evaluation. Implementers shall establish a versioned applicability configuration for each intended use, specifying data types, mandatory modalities, metric definitions, thresholds, inspection methods, and evidence requirements. Appendices A and B list the operator IDs, catalog names, criteria, and evidence covered by this project's reference implementation. Implementers may use validated equivalent methods and shall supply the evidence required for the declared use. Authorization, complex physical consistency, task semantics, and model performance also require the independent evidence specified in the relevant sections. The references provide research and methodological background. See the operator catalog (catalog.yaml) for implementation variants and operator versions, and the full-suite plan (all.yaml) for the default plan.

2.3 Applicability configuration and threshold selection

Each applicability configuration shall include at least:

1. Target model use, tasks, and deployment conditions; data sources, devices, embodiments, and control modes.

2. Required and optional modalities; units, coordinate systems, time anchors, sampling frequencies, and action semantics.

3. Each metric's statistical objects, calculation method, threshold, comparison operator, minimum evidence, and inapplicability conditions.

4. Calibration data version, human reference judgments, false-positive and false-negative assessments, allowed measurement error, and scope.

5. Dataset distribution and effective scale targets, sampling plan, anomaly handling, and acceptance responsibilities.

6. A mapping from applicable main-text clauses to inspection methods and acceptance evidence, with reasons for inapplicable clauses.

7. The unified data view's semantic version, input object and capability requirements, field and stream bindings, and evidence links between native and processed data. Record storage formats, readers, and adapter versions separately in ingestion records; they do not define the applicability of semantic quality checks.

8. For each supervision use, specify mandatory checks, the objects affected by masks, the gating reference axis, interval mappings and denominators, limits on defect proportion and continuous duration, and the effect of sampling exclusions on the amount actually used.

9. Full or sampled verification scope, sampling completion criteria, handling of unverified and review items, and the primary gating and supplementary checks for each quality phenomenon, avoiding double counting or conflicting rules.

Thresholds shall reflect device accuracy, task speed, critical-event duration, and model training windows. Define time, pixel distance, physical distance, and angle separately. Image thresholds shall state resolution, bit depth, color space, and distortion treatment. Distinguish measurement error from an agreed acceptance margin: the former derives from data precision or measurement error; the latter from the user's explicitly agreed use and permitted deviation. An acceptance margin must not be described as device measurement accuracy or a physical safety margin. For both, record the value, unit, applicable quantity, basis, and configuration version. Successful computation does not verify the basis of a tolerance. Preserve original configurations and historical reports after changes, recalculating or reinspecting affected scope; do not derive tolerances solely from a target pass rate. See §4.6 for original limits versus acceptance boundaries in joint-state envelope checks.

Source authenticity, usage authorization, required modalities, action semantics, data lineage, and split isolation must not be waived by adjusting numeric thresholds or omitting checks. The configuration shall cover every applicable main-text requirement; choosing only easy-to-pass checks cannot establish conformity.

Define pass conditions for every applicable requirement. Where detectors yield only candidate anomalies, also define insufficient-evidence states and review workflows. Keep threshold calibration and method validation appropriately separate from final acceptance samples, and report applicable scenarios, reference ground truth, false positives, and false negatives. Before comparing quality metrics across organizations, confirm that configurations, denominators, and inspection methods are comparable.

For video-only, single-camera, or force-sensor-free data, identify absent modalities and inapplicable items. A missing signal declared mandatory must not be marked not applicable. Applicability depends jointly on data type, supervision objective, and delivery agreement; a missing sensor or inability to inspect does not waive an existing commitment.

2.5 Unified temporal data view and format decoupling

Define quality requirements in terms of content and semantics. Semantic quality checks shall not depend on file extensions, container layouts, or a particular intermediate storage format. When adapters provide semantically equivalent objects from different formats, use the same methods and verdict rules. Ego, physical robot, and UMI describe data types; operator applicability depends jointly on declared use, required modalities, signal semantics, and evidence conditions.

Temporal structure is the backbone of embodied interaction data, supplemented by static or interval context such as calibration, robot models, units, coordinates, provenance, and task definitions. Do not reduce every object to equal-length two-dimensional arrays at one frequency. Static assets also require appropriate structural, numerical, and semantic checks.

2.5.3 Capability declarations and consistency validation

Every semantic quality check shall declare required objects, shape and semantic constraints, timelines, validity requirements, and full or sampled inspection scope. Finiteness checks need numeric values and validity semantics; sampling-gap checks need native timelines; projection checks additionally need camera models, transforms, and projected objects. The existence of a temporal array does not make every check executable.

The adaptation layer shall distinguish “truly absent,” “present but unbound,” “unsupported by the reader,” “readable but insufficiently evidenced,” and “read failure.” For specification acceptance, missing, unbound, or unsupported required fields, files, timelines, or other objects shall produce missing/insufficient-evidence warnings (warn), reasons, and affected requirements. Mark unperformed work unverified and preserve measured scope for inspected portions. If required verification cannot be completed, acceptance for that use is incomplete; a warning cannot authorize release. Mere absence does not automatically isolate the whole record. Confirmed absent objects still count toward asset-completeness omissions, but a missing-evidence warning is not confirmed illegal values or corruption.

This describes acceptance after evidence review; it does not imply that all raw operator reports already return warn. Preserve actual raw statuses, reasons, and evidence. Some implementations may currently return fail, execution error, or not applicable. At acceptance, review reasons and configuration under §7.2 and record verification and acceptance states separately, without overwriting raw results or treating missing evidence as confirmed corruption.

Where no applicable implementation can be selected or prerequisites block inspection, record skipped and the reason. Missing implementation does not make a requirement inapplicable. Inapplicability must follow the previously declared use, modalities, and conditions; inability to inspect does not waive a commitment. None of these situations counts as pass or complete. If original evidence does not accompany processed artifacts, link traceable source-check results or supplemental evidence rather than inferring that the source object never existed.

Provided objects that are empty, corrupt, non-finite, temporally reversed, length-inconsistent, or outside declared thresholds shall be recorded as confirmed defects under the relevant criteria, without downgrading them because other items are missing. Tool, permission, resource, or dependency failures are execution errors; invalid inspection configuration is a configuration error. Neither is a data defect. Record object availability, verification completion, and acceptance status separately under §7.2.

Given equivalent times, values, validity, and context, semantic checks on different storage representations shall produce consistent conclusions and equivalent evidence under the same configuration. Adapter validation shall cover units, coordinates, clock domains, missing and duplicate data, non-finite values, variable-length streams, and cross-axis mappings. Declare numerical tolerances in advance. If encoding or lossy processing changes content, shared provenance alone does not establish equivalent input.

The “input data objects” columns in Appendices A and B define dependencies, not a claim that any tool has completed unified-view adaptation. List implementation coverage, adapter support, status mappings, and validation separately in versioned implementation documentation and inspection reports. A storage implementation must not become a specification-wide restriction. If tool output differs from acceptance requirements, preserve raw results and record blocked requirements from actual evidence under §7.2; do not infer that missing objects were verified or motion exceeded limits.

3 Relationship between high-quality embodied data and WAM capabilities

3.1 From interaction records to prediction and action

WAMs learn future changes and corresponding actions from historical observations and task conditions. DreamZero illustrates the potential of joint video/action modeling for transfer across tasks, environments, and embodiments. Its results apply to specific models and experimental settings, not a universal performance guarantee for WAMs. World Action Models are Zero-shot Policies

Accordingly, reliable interaction records are central to WAM data development: temporal misalignment breaks action–outcome correspondence; coordinate or scale errors distort spatial change; incorrect instructions and outcome labels contaminate supervision; broken long sequences weaken learning of multistep processes. Clear images are only one condition and cannot replace cross-modal consistency or evidence of real physical interaction.

Good embodied data provides trustworthy interaction evidence for the declared learning objective, with sufficient coverage and effective experience in target scenarios. Individual records shall be authentic, complete, spatiotemporally consistent, and semantically clear. Datasets shall offer use-appropriate diversity, effective scale, and independent evaluation conditions.

High-quality embodied data shall accurately record what occurred, including success, failure, retries, takeovers, and recovery. Distinguish task failure from data corruption. Authentic, interpretable failures can support dynamics prediction, failure detection, and recovery learning; suitability for behavioral imitation depends on objectives and labels.

Clear images, high task success, or many collection hours alone cannot prove quality. Evaluation shall address reliable learning signals, target coverage, sufficient effective experience, and transparent limitations together.

The claim that “high-quality data improves WAM performance” shall be testable: under comparable models, training budgets, and independent evaluations, assess how quality improvements, distribution gaps filled, and effective scale growth contribute to prediction, control, and generalization. High-quality embodied data is an important foundation for reliable learning; actual gains also depend on model capacity, training method, and target tasks.

4 Data quality requirements

4.1 Provenance, authorization, and traceability

Record data sources, collector identity or anonymous ID, device IDs, collection time and method, authorized usage scope, and version. Distinguish real collection, simulation, synthesis, automatic annotation, and human revision. Data with fabricated provenance, confused action-label sources, or unverifiable usage authorization shall not enter the corresponding training or release workflow.

Establish sensitive-information identification, necessary de-identification, access controls, and review records appropriate to usage and delivery scope. Authorization and privacy review are admission conditions across collection, processing, and delivery. Include de-identification's effects on manipulation regions, visual occlusion, and supervision information in quality evaluation.

4.2 Structural, modality, and metadata completeness

Provide unique episode IDs, raw-record links, file inventories, integrity checksums, modality descriptions, and processing versions. Supply task, outcome, and calibration information as required by use, explicitly marking inapplicable items. Required files shall be readable and required declared intervals fully decodable. Timestamps, frame counts, shapes, indices, and metadata shall agree.

Verify that video, state, and action streams originate from the same collection session and corresponding interval, with correct camera roles, left/right end-effector assignments, and channel bindings. Matching filenames, lengths, or motion correlations alone cannot prove correct pairing. For repeated tasks or similar trajectories, cross-check source IDs, binding manifests, and independently visible events. Check referenced source sessions, intervals, and channels across deliverables, preserving evidence of cross-episode or left/right mismatches. Insufficient pairing evidence requires review; incomplete mandatory pairing verification cannot count as qualified for that use. Correlation candidates are not confirmed mismatches.

Label missing, invalid, interpolated, padded, and model-estimated values separately. Zeros or repeated frames must not conceal missing data, and successful conversion alone does not establish quality. Scan all required numeric values declared valid for finiteness. Checking a few rows cannot establish the absence of NaN or Inf elsewhere. Reconcile coverage against an inventory of logical objects for states, actions, and related numeric assets. Record the objects and element counts expected and checked, and explain omissions. The scan may run in blocks. Dedicated checks may cover static numeric objects such as calibration, provided they appear in the same coverage inventory. Verify the validity mask declaration and its correspondence to the data. Masks must not be rewritten after inspection to conceal anomalies.

4.3 Temporal synchronization and causal correspondence

Preserve native collection timestamps and declare clock sources, units, synchronization, drift compensation, and resampling mappings. Check camera-to-camera synchronization, camera-to-state synchronization, and action-command/measured-response correspondence separately. Equal frame counts do not prove synchronization.

Camera timestamps shall identify exposure, capture, or reception time. Record relevant parameters or measurement limitations when rolling shutter or other effects influence critical-event timing accuracy. Modalities may retain different native rates; acceptance verifies time mappings and target-window coverage rather than equal frame counts.

Action records shall state time anchors for command issuance, control effect, or state transitions, with available latency evidence. Preserve derivation methods for state-derived actions. Action/state counts and corresponding intervals shall follow the declared interface, without requiring every action representation to have the same count as state frames.

Inputs for a decision at time t must not contain future information unavailable at deployment. Future observations may be prediction targets or explicitly documented offline label sources, but must not masquerade as online inputs. Audit the information scope used by interpolation, smoothing, and labeling. Record input field provenance, processing windows, availability times, and prediction-target boundaries. Separate labels using future samples from online-available inputs. Missing processing lineage means unverified; timestamps no later than the decision time alone do not prove absence of future-information leakage.

Report quantiles of time offsets, the proportion exceeding tolerance, the longest continuous gap, and the critical events affected. Training windows must not cross unexplained recording breaks or backward time jumps. Preserve the original records and mappings when masking or trimming is necessary.

For signals at different sampling rates, build traceable alignment using native timing and report errors, valid coverage, and unmatched intervals. Alignment and resampling shall preserve the semantics of continuous values, discrete states, rotations, and events. Out-of-tolerance intervals must not count as valid synchronized supervision.

4.4 Spatial calibration and physical consistency

This section applies to uses involving poses, projection, 3D geometry, or spatial fusion across sensors.

Specify coordinate systems, length and angle units, axes, handedness, rotation representations, and transform direction. Camera intrinsics/extrinsics shall match the device, resolution, imaging model, and collection period. Reconfirm calibration applicability after installation changes.

Check both mathematical validity and observation consistency. An invertible rigid transform does not establish correct extrinsic direction. Results derived from one estimation chain are not independent verification. Reprojection shall use traceable 3D–2D correspondences and report valid observation coverage. First check direction against declared source/target frames and the transform chain, then validate against aligned independent observations. FK may provide a 3D reference only with correct embodiment, base, and camera relationships. Comparing in-frame projection rates for forward and inverse transforms yields direction-error candidates only, not sufficient failure evidence. Confirm a declared-direction mismatch only with sufficient independent correspondences, synchronization, imaging model, observation count, and predefined error criteria. If neither hypothesis explains observations or evidence is insufficient, request review rather than automatically inverting or rewriting extrinsics.

Express 2D errors in pixels or image-normalized units, and 3D errors in declared physical units. Convert between them only with explicit depth, geometry, and error-propagation conditions. Set joint, end-effector, and gripper envelopes for the relevant device and task; do not interchange human-hand, UMI-trajectory, and physical-robot limits.

4.5 Observation quality and visibility of critical interactions

Inspect video decodability, frozen frames, exposure, blur, compression distortion, occlusion, and actual sampling cadence. Evaluation should focus on end effectors, objects, contact regions, and critical events, rather than relying solely on whole-frame mean brightness or whole-clip anomaly rates.

Distinguish device faults from genuine difficult conditions. Natural occlusion, low light, stationary waiting, and stable grasping may belong to the target distribution; assess them against task objectives. For multiple cameras, report critical-event observability in at least one usable view where possible.

Inspection plans shall cover critical stages and events. Checks requiring adjacent samples, including continuous freezing, gaps, cadence, and longest anomaly duration, shall by default cover the complete declared interval in acceptance mode. Streaming chunks may be used while preserving adjacency across chunks. Sparse independent frames cannot replace continuity verification. For metrics allowing sampling, such as exposure and sharpness, use a predefined stratified plan spanning the beginning, middle, end, and known critical events, stating the conditions for statistical inference.

Completing a declared, applicable sampling plan may count as “complete (sampled),” recording sampling units, strata, counts, temporal coverage, and inference scope. It does not mean every frame passed. A prefix-only full-inspection run, unfinished sampling plan, or uncovered critical stage is partial completion with a warning; mandatory acceptance remains incomplete. Prefix mode is for quick diagnosis only. Preserve candidates and confirmed defects, but infer whole-clip exceedance from local anomaly rates only with a justified sampling basis. Neither full nor sampled inspection can claim verification beyond directly inspected or validly inferred scope.

Spatial resolution and sampling frequency shall suffice to identify target events. Validate the effects of downsampling, cropping, denoising, and compression on contact instants, fast motion, small objects, and long-sequence continuity. There is no universal 30 Hz requirement.

For depth maps or point clouds, declare scale, coordinate system, valid range, invalid values, and measured or estimated provenance. Check omissions, outliers, and cross-modal registration. Derived data such as monocular depth estimates shall record scale basis and uncertainty and must not be delivered as directly measured ground truth.

4.6 Action, state, and contact signal quality

Check action/state validity, continuity, ranges, index correspondence, and continuous anomalous intervals. Per-frame envelope checks may report abnormal frame counts, rates, and longest runs. Acceptance based on actual duration additionally requires real timelines and interval support; frame counts are not seconds. Preserve commands and measured states separately. Large commanded motion with little displacement may result from blocked contact and does not alone prove incorrect labels.

For derived actions, record coordinate transformations, differencing intervals, filtering, clipping, and compensation methods. When smoothing or clipping values, retain the originals, processing parameters, and a mask of changes. Verify whether the operation altered endpoints, contact events, or dynamic characteristics.

Label action supervision derived from hand poses or inter-finger distances as estimated. Declare the extraction method, validity mask, and mapping error. Such labels must not be presented as measured robot controls. Hand observations that fail inspection cannot yield trusted action labels. The accompanying video may still be evaluated against independent observation-quality requirements.

Where IMU, force/torque, tactile, or chassis signals exist, inspect bias, noise, saturation, drift, and timing alignment. Claims of contact-dynamics supervision shall state observable evidence and limitations. Data without corresponding sensors must not claim directly measured force or tactile ground truth.

Contact force inferred from motor current, drive-side torque, joint effort, or gripper opening shall be labeled a proxy or estimate, recording original quantities, units, transmission and geometry mappings, calibration data, and error bounds. Uncalibrated drive torque must not be labeled fingertip force in newtons or used for universal cross-device force thresholds. Force/torque bias and drift apply only with clear units, contact state, and gravity-compensation conventions. Stationary does not mean contact-free, and drive-side no-load readings are not automatically F/T sensor zero bias.

Stationarity, low speed, and holding an object are interaction states, not inherent defects. Use task phase, participating components, and independent evidence to distinguish normal states, use mismatch, and device faults. Record sampling-policy exclusions separately from actual defects; reduced sampling weight does not mean corruption.

Evaluate motion according to physical meaning and representation, accounting for sampling rate, noise, and processing. Do not combine incomparable translation, rotation, or differently unitized signals into one numerical verdict. Rotation jumps should use geodesic angles between relative rotations and actual time intervals; component differences are diagnostics with stated limitations. Validate state-derived actions by representation: Euclidean increments against adjacent state differences, pose increments by transform composition. Actual control commands need not equal measured state differences.

Relative outlier scores based on a clip's median and dispersion generate candidates only. They cannot independently prove physical violations or plausible absolute change throughout the clip. For gating, provide a device/dataset baseline calibrated on independent data, its scope, and a lower bound on change with clear units or scale; record how degenerate baselines are handled. Near-zero dispersion must not amplify tiny numerical errors. Hard workspace and joint limits require traceable provenance. List measurement tolerances or agreed acceptance margins separately under §2.3; a relaxed pass does not mean original limits were never exceeded. Quantiles cannot replace a declared maximum-value constraint.

Joint-state envelope checks shall preserve both original limits and acceptance boundaries. With an outward absolute tolerance, position boundaries are “lower limit minus tolerance through upper limit plus tolerance”; velocity and torque boundaries are their respective absolute limits plus tolerance. Declare comparison operators, units, and physical quantities in the configuration. A position acceptance margin does not automatically apply to velocity, torque, or control commands. Without a configured tolerance, no allowed exceedance may be assumed. Reports shall separately record the original limit source, model and joint-mapping versions, units and zero-reference basis, measurement error or acceptance margin and its rationale, reporting exceedance magnitude and affected scope relative to original limits as well as the verdict relative to acceptance boundaries. An original-limit exceedance does not automatically prove device failure; passing relaxed boundaries proves neither safe operation nor compliance with original hard limits. Physical executability or safety evaluation requires separate review against device and task requirements. Missing original exceedance statistics, zero-reference evidence, or limit provenance shall be recorded as insufficient evidence; a relaxed pass cannot substitute for them.

With sufficient skeleton definitions, point order, units, and reference ranges, confirmed keypoint-estimation failure is a defect for geometry supervision and dependent derived actions. Include it in defect and valid-duration statistics for those uses; do not relabel it a sampling-policy exclusion. Without references or with insufficient temporal clues, preserve audit candidates without claiming skeleton accuracy. Evaluate video, independent commands, and other supervision separately.

4.7 Task semantics, annotations, and outcome consistency

When supplying task or language supervision, define objectives, manipulated objects, constraints, start/end boundaries, and outcome criteria. Long tasks should include stages and critical events. Use consistent definitions for success, failure, partial completion, unknown, and human takeover; takeover intervals must not count as autonomous success.

Automatic or model-assisted annotation shall record the model, prompt template, version, and meaning of confidence scores, validating reliability on independent human-reviewed samples. A model score of 0.95 is not a verified 95% accuracy. Critical outcomes should be confirmed from continuous processes and necessary sensor evidence; use human review if a few sampled frames are insufficient.

Human annotation shall provide guidelines, review proportions, and disagreement resolution, reporting agreement and sampled error. Syntactically valid text is not necessarily semantically correct. Language, sentence length, or expression style alone must not determine low quality.

For multilabel classification, report label incidence and corresponding data amounts, avoiding summing labels as sample counts. List unknown/missing labels separately, recording classification methods, versions, and sampled agreement.

5.3 Joint coverage and long-tail support

Single-dimension class counts do not establish combined capability coverage. Select critical combinations based on target capabilities, such as task × material, skill × embodiment, scene × lighting, or failure mode × recovery strategy. Count valid duration, deduplicated episodes, and collection sessions per unit.

Coverage = target units meeting their minimum effective support / the target unit set predefined in the applicability configuration. Report uncovered and under-supported units. One occurrence does not automatically provide sufficient coverage. Additional deployment-frequency- or risk-weighted coverage may be reported with disclosed weights and rationale.

Prioritize long-tail gaps with clear task value. Valid failures, recoveries, disturbances, and varied initial states help characterize interaction boundaries. Do not systematically remove them merely to raise overall qualification or demonstration success rates.

5.4 Mixing, sampling, and distribution shift

Report raw, post-inspection, and actual training sampling distributions separately. State mixing ratios, measurement units, and sampling weights for ego, physical-robot, UMI, simulated, and synthetic data. Reconcile action and modality semantics before assigning training objectives to different sources.

Analyze whether filtering disproportionately removes fast motion, low light, occlusion, specific embodiments, or failure/recovery samples. If quality processing removes target units, collect replacements or revise use, recording reasons. Higher sampling weights alone do not add deduplicated effective data to a unit.

Before training, compare target and delivered distributions where possible. At minimum report major class proportions, uncovered units, maximum source share, and group differences. Distribution distances shall state classification granularity, smoothing, and threshold provenance; a single distance does not replace coverage review.

Report usable scale and distribution separately for video, action, and joint supervision, each with its own validity mask. Missing or unreliable action labels need not exclude otherwise usable video. Padded actions must be excluded from the action loss. This division of uses draws on Dyna-2's organization of video and action data. Its benefits require validation in the target setting. 5.5 Dataset splits and leakage control

Isolate training, validation, and test sets at traceable source level. Segments, multiple views, resampled, transcoded, augmented, and near-duplicate derivatives of one raw recording shall share one split group.

Hold out locations, object instances, task combinations, or embodiments according to the generalization being tested, and disclose rules. Random frames from the same scene cannot alone prove cross-environment generalization. For cross-embodiment claims, disclose whether the target embodiment participated in pretraining or adaptation.

Freeze and version split manifests. Use validation for threshold/policy selection; do not repeatedly tune on independent tests. After test-driven data improvements, use new independent evaluation or disclose reuse. Also state known overlap risks with external pretraining data.

6.2 Effective scale calculation and deduplication

For the target use, first identify intervals satisfying required modalities, quality, authorization, and continuity; then deduplicate and merge intervals. Sum deduplicated valid duration by original session ID and interval union. Do not count one interval repeatedly across files, cameras, or windows.

Use traceable time intervals and declare open/closed conventions and last-sample duration handling. Irregular or gapped samples cannot be measured simply as frame count / nominal frame rate. Independent simultaneous interactions by different collectors may be counted separately.

Record exact duplicates, overlapping same-source data, and reviewed near-duplicates separately. Repeated real trials of one task may offer independent variation; semantic similarity alone is insufficient for deletion. Preserve cluster IDs, retention rules, thresholds, sampled false-match reviews, and excluded volume.

Derived windows do not add original duration. Cropping, augmentation, and repeated sampling increase supply or consumption, not deduplicated effective scale. Deduplicated records may still have temporal/session correlations; also disclose independent session counts and scene support.

6.3 Continuous windows and long-sequence scale

Count usable training windows using the declared history length, prediction horizon, sampling interval, stride, and validity mask. A window must contain all modalities it requires and must not cross recording breaks or invalid intervals. Padding does not count as a valid observation. Where windows overlap, report their count, overlap, and effective interaction duration covered. Window count alone does not measure independent interactions.

For long sequences, separately report continuous valid-duration distributions, multistep task counts, critical-event coverage, and complete task-chain proportions. Many short clips do not automatically replace long-sequence experience. Irrelevant waiting in long videos should not all count as effective manipulation, but task-required waiting, observation, and stable holding shall remain.

6.4 Expansion strategy and marginal benefit

This specification sets no universal WAM minimum hours, trajectories, or data volume. Determine scale targets from model capacity, training budget, task complexity, distribution support, and independent evaluation.

First satisfy quality and support in critical distribution units, then expand progressively where possible. Robotic imitation-learning research finds that environment/object diversity matters in its studied tasks and that more demonstrations under one condition may show diminishing returns. Validate the conclusion on the target WAM before using it for collection decisions. Data Scaling Laws in Imitation Learning for Robotic Manipulation

Each expansion should report added valid duration, independent scenes/objects/tasks, changes in critical-unit coverage, and cost per effective data unit, evaluating marginal benefits under §8. When performance plateaus, analyze quality, distribution, scale, and training-budget constraints separately rather than only collecting more repetitions.

7.4 Sampling, review, and batch acceptance

Fully inspect required asset structure, numeric validity, and index contracts within declared expected scope. For video observation quality, distinguish continuous metrics from sample-eligible ones under §4.5. Semantics, annotation, and complex physical consistency may use predefined sampling specifying population, units, strata, size, acceptable error, and confidence level, including rare classes. Review suspected anomalies separately; do not mix targeted reviews into unweighted population error estimates.

Account for within-session correlation rather than treating adjacent frames as many independent observations. Systematic issues require broader reinspection across related devices, calibrations, annotation versions, or batches, tracing delivered artifacts and updating conclusions. No observed errors in a sample does not mean no population errors.

Separate candidate-review samples from samples estimating population error. Unequal-probability stratified sampling requires design-weighted estimates; a pooled error rate cannot directly support population inference. Reports shall distinguish full inspection from sampled inference and must not generate per-check passes for individually uninspected episodes.

Batch acceptance requires frozen configuration; completed, satisfied sample- and dataset-level mandatory checks; disclosed warnings and evidence limitations; agreed distribution/effective-scale targets; traceable splits and lineage; and complete delivery materials. Warning-bearing data meeting mandatory requirements may be delivered for the declared use but must not be relabeled pass. High scores elsewhere cannot offset serious source errors, missing authorization, critical action-semantic errors, or train/test leakage.

Batches authorized under predefined sampling may count qualified scale based on batch acceptance, identifying which requirements use full checks and which use inference. Per-sample manifests shall state sampling inclusion and link batch reports. Unsampled samples are not expected check instances for that sampled item and must not be falsely marked inspected/pass.

Providers shall submit configurations, evidence, and defect handling; acceptance parties shall review scope and conformity claims. Reports shall assign preparation, review, and acceptance responsibilities. Preserve original evidence and adjudication reasons for disputed samples; reinspect under the same configuration where needed.

7.5 Conformity statements

Limit statements to dataset version, use, configuration, inspection and sampling scope, and known limitations. For example: “Dataset version X satisfies this specification under configuration Y for use Z.” If mandatory requirements are not all covered, list partial conformity and unmet items rather than claiming full conformity.

A qualified sample does not establish dataset coverage; accepted datasets do not establish validated model performance. Provide separate §8 evidence for the latter.

7.6 Per-sample inspection records

Deliver machine-readable per-episode manifests linked to input version and applicability configuration. Each check shall at least record requirement, method/version, execution result, verification completion and full/sampled scope, quality verdict, acceptance by use, measurements/units, thresholds, evidence references, and disposition. Nonquantifiable human judgments shall record rationale and review responsibility.

For a defect that can be localized, record the affected camera or signal channel, original time interval, and corresponding frame indices, transition indices, or Boolean mask. Specify the time base, index origin, endpoint convention, and mask length. For missing original samples, record the expected time and available neighboring source indices. A frame index introduced by resampling must not be presented as an original index.

Record completion separately from pass/warn/fail. An empty anomaly list does not prove completion. Window exclusions, cropping, repair, or smoothing shall link outputs to raw data, conversion versions, changed intervals, and reinspection so training users can reproduce selection. 8.3 Reproducibility and limits of conclusions

Record model/initialization, known pretraining scope, training configuration, sampling weights, seeds, checkpoint selection, test version, and robot/control configuration. Providers lacking model-validation access may deliver quality, distribution, and scale evidence with an explicit “model performance not yet validated” statement. Passing inspection, many hours, or numerous checks cannot replace performance-gain evidence.

9 Delivery, versioning, and continuous improvement

9.1 Minimum deliverables

Each delivery shall include a dataset card, asset/checksum inventory, schema and modality descriptions, applicability configuration and clause-to-evidence mapping, calibration/coordinate documentation, lineage, inspection/review reports, distribution and scale statistics, split manifests, and usage scope. Repairs, relabeling, deduplication, or use changes require change manifests and impact scope. Performance claims require corresponding evaluations.

Reports shall be reproducible from the same data version and configuration. Processing completion, generated artifacts, and successful inspection execution are separate from acceptance and cannot replace quality, distribution, or effective-scale evidence.

9.2 Continuous improvement

Establish a collection → inspection → distribution analysis → training → independent evaluation → gap-filling loop where possible. Map model feedback to tasks, scenes, and defect types before deciding repairs, relabeling, collection, or sampling changes. Recompute quality, distribution, and effective scale after each filter/repair to avoid better metrics at the expense of critical coverage.

9.3 Version maintenance and change responsibilities

When definitions, scope, methods, or thresholds change, record reasons, impact, and reinspection needs. Validate input requirements, applicability, measurement error, and review plans before adopting new acceptance methods. Method/tool updates shall not automatically change historical conformity.

Link every record to collection, definition, processing, and inspection versions. Mixed-version datasets shall declare compatibility and identify samples needing reprocessing.
